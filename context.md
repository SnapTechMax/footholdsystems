# FootHold context

Everything known about the business as of 25 August 2026, written so a person
or a model can pick up the work without re-deriving it.

**Read this first.** Two different kinds of statement live in here and mixing
them up is how bad copy gets written:

- **Known** — observed, in the code, or stated directly by Max.
- **Assumed** — a hypothesis that reached the live site because it was written
  into approved copy. Plausible, unvalidated, and marked as such.

Anything genuinely unknown is marked `[UNSURE]` rather than guessed at. There is
no customer research behind this document. Nobody has interviewed a buyer.

## company.md

### What the company sells

Answer Engine Optimization. The one-line offer, in Max's words:

> I help your business show up on ChatGPT when potential customers ask.

Concretely: making a business findable, readable and recommendable to AI
assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, Google AI Overviews) at the
moment someone describes a problem and asks who to hire.

Entry point is a **free AI visibility scan**. Someone submits a URL, an email
and a business category; the site scans them, scores them, and emails a
plain-English report. The paid tiers sell the fixes and then the implementation.

### Who it serves

Three categories are built into the product, and each is scored against a
different check set:

| Category | Who |
|---|---|
| `sbo` | Local and service businesses: trades, clinics, agencies, restaurants |
| `ecommerce` | Sells physical or digital products online |
| `saas` | Sells software, an app, or an API |

`sbo` is the default on the form and the assumed majority of cold traffic.

**Assumed** (from approved sales copy, not research): businesses with real
margin where one customer is worth hundreds or thousands rather than twelve
dollars; customers who research before buying; an established business with
real reviews and history.

### How it makes money

Three tiers. Detail in `offer.md`. Summary: **$49** for the fix list, **$1,500**
for implementation plus a purpose-built second domain, **$2,000 + $2,500/month
on a six-month minimum** for the ongoing retainer.

The retainer is a **separate funnel**, sold only to people who have already
bought the $1,500. It must not appear in top-of-funnel copy.

`[UNSURE]` Whether any tier has been sold yet. No revenue figures, no customer
count, no delivery-capacity constraints are known.

### What it believes

These are load-bearing. Most of the copy follows from them.

- **Search is going winner-take-all.** Google returned ten results and fourth
  place still got clicks. An assistant returns one recommendation. You are not
  second; you are absent.
- **Models recommend entities, not pages.** Ranking #1 on Google does not make
  a model believe you are the business to call. Document matching and reputation
  are different problems.
- **A model does not trust your website about your website.** It weighs
  corroboration. Silence does not read as neutral, it reads as unproven.
- **Vague businesses are unrecommendable.** Not disliked. Unmatched to any
  question.
- **Nobody controls a model's output.** Anyone guaranteeing a ranking is lying.
  What is controllable is every input the model uses.
- **The loss is invisible.** No impression, no bounce, no line in Analytics. The
  first marketing channel that is undetectable to the business losing it.
- **Give the tactics away.** The 22-email sequence hands over every technical
  fix for free. The value sold is judgement and implementation, not information.

### What makes it different

- **The second domain.** The $1,500 builds a separate site whose only audience
  is machines. An existing site has to sell to people and carry a brand, and
  every one of those pulls against machine readability. Max's framing: nobody
  else is doing this.
- **Category-specific scoring.** Most tools score every site against one list.
  Scoring a party rental company on whether it ships an SDK is nonsense; scoring
  a SaaS company without it is flattery.
- **A free scan that is genuinely useful**, delivered before any ask.
- **`.gov` backlinks** in the retainer. Max's framing: "no one is doing this."
- **A refund-scale guarantee** on the retainer: not ranking on LLM engines after
  180 days means Max pays $15,000.

## customer.md

**Everything in this section is assumed unless marked otherwise.** It comes from
copy that Max approved, not from talking to buyers. Treat it as the current
hypothesis and overwrite it the moment real customers say otherwise.

### ICP

Owner-operators and small marketing decision-makers who already have a working
business and a website that predates any of this. Someone who has spent money on
SEO or ads before and can tell the difference between a channel and a gimmick.

Sharpest fit: a local service business with real margin, real reviews, and a
site built for humans by somebody who is no longer involved.

`[UNSURE]` Company size, revenue band, geography beyond an implied US/California
default, industry concentration.

### Pains

- Leads quietly declining with no diagnosable cause.
- Having paid for SEO for years and not knowing whether it still works.
- Watching a competitor get named and having no idea why.
- Being unable to answer "is this a real channel or hype?"
- No time. Everything on the fix list is easy and none of it is urgent, which is
  exactly why it never gets done.

### Objections

Answered directly on the sales page:

1. *"Isn't this too early?"*
2. *"Can you guarantee ChatGPT will recommend me?"* — answered **no**, on purpose.
3. *"I already rank number one on Google."*
4. *"Won't AI just work out who is genuinely best?"*
5. *"Can't I just do this myself?"* — answered **yes, partly**, on purpose.

**Observed, not assumed:** a real reader disputed his scan result because he
ranks #1 for his own name and the report implied otherwise. The objection
"your tool is wrong about me" is live and has already happened once.

### Buying triggers

`[UNSURE]` — none observed. Reasonable hypotheses only:

- Reading their own scan and seeing a competitor named instead of them.
- A grade lower than expected on a site they are proud of.
- Someone mentioning they found a competitor via ChatGPT.

### Questions they ask

From the site FAQ, so assumed:

- What exactly do I get from the free scan?
- How long does it take to see movement?
- Do I have to stop doing SEO?
- Does this work for local service businesses?
- Is there a contract?

### Language

`[UNSURE]` — no transcripts, no recordings, no support inbox to mine. This is
the largest genuine gap in this document. The copy currently uses language
written *for* the customer rather than language captured *from* them.

What is known: the report deliberately avoids Ora's engineer-facing vocabulary,
because "publish an Agentic Resource Discovery catalog" means nothing to a
roofer.

### Fears

- Paying for something unmeasurable.
- Being sold hype by another agency.
- Being told to rebuild a website they just paid for.
- Looking foolish for not having heard of this.

### Decision criteria

- Can they see the problem themselves? The scan exists to make it self-evident.
- Is the number credible? A grade contradicted by their own experience destroys
  trust instantly, which is why the grade cap and the cold-search caveat exist.
- Do they keep the work? Both domains and a written record of what changed.
- Is it a retainer? The $1,500 is deliberately one-off.

## offer.md

### Packages

**Tier 1 — $49. "Here's how to fix what the scan found."**
The prioritised fixes from their own scan. Sold on the report page and in the
report email, at the moment the fixes are blurred in front of them. Impulse
purchase at peak context.

**Tier 2 — $1,500, one-off. Two to three weeks.**
- Every technical fix from the scan, implemented on the existing site
- Pages rewritten, positioning decided first, in their voice
- **Listing consistency**: Google Business Profile, trade directories and
  profiles they already own, made to agree on name, address, phone and claims
- **A second site on its own domain**, built for machines only
- They keep everything, both domains plus a written record of what changed

**Tier 3 — $2,000 up front, then $2,500/month, six-month minimum.**
Separate funnel. Only sold to existing $1,500 customers.
- 2x social posts from influencers AI is listening to
- High-trust `.gov` backlinks
- Monthly query tracking
- New answer content monthly
- Review generation system with an SOP for staff
- Monthly high-trust Reddit posts
- **Guarantee:** not ranking on LLM engines after 180 days → Max pays $15,000

### Pricing logic

- **$49** is priced as an impulse, below the threshold where anyone needs
  approval. Framed as less than an hour of most people's billable time.
- **$1,500** is one-off on purpose. "Not a retainer" is a selling point against
  agencies. Framed against the value of a single commercial job.
- **Tier 3** is where recurring work lives, because recurring work has recurring
  cost. The guarantee is affordable precisely because it only applies to people
  who already did tier 2.

**The tiers must never be blurred.** Monthly tracking, new content, link
acquisition and review systems belong to tier 3 only. Putting any of them in the
$1,500 promises ongoing work for a one-off fee. This has already been caught
once in the copy.

### Proof

Thin, and worth being honest about.

- **Known:** footholdsystems.com scored **20/100, grade F** on its own scan.
  Reproducible. Used as the opening of the email sequence.
- **Known:** foreverpartyrentals.com scored **91/100, grade B** as a local
  business, and **42/100, grade D** had it been scored as SaaS. Demonstrates the
  category system.
- **Known:** across roughly a dozen sites scanned, the developer-tooling checks
  were irrelevant to every non-software business.
- `[UNSURE]` No testimonials. No case studies. No before/after ranking data. No
  client results of any kind.

### Promises

- A written report showing what the answer engines say, who is named instead,
  and which signals are failing.
- No call required, no card, for the scan.
- Tier 2: implementation in two to three weeks, everything handed over.
- Tier 3 only: the $15,000 / 180-day guarantee.

### Claims to avoid

- **Any guaranteed ranking or recommendation.** The copy says outright that
  nobody controls a model's output and anyone claiming otherwise is lying. Never
  contradict this.
- **Invented statistics.** No fabricated percentages, adoption figures or
  "93% of buyers" claims. The site currently contains none, deliberately. If a
  number is used it must be sourced.
- **Monthly anything in tiers 1 or 2.**
- **Affiliation or partnership** with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Perplexity or
  Anthropic.
- **Overstating a scan result.** A partial pass is not a failure. Saying "your
  name doesn't bring up your website" to someone who ranks #1 destroyed trust
  once already.
- **Implying the scan reflects a personalised search.** It is a cold search: no
  login, no history, no location. The report says so.
- **Fabricated case studies or testimonials.** There are none. Do not write one.

### Good-fit customer

Yes:
- Real margin; one customer worth hundreds or thousands
- Customers research before buying
- Real business, real reviews, real work to point at
- Willing to move while the category is empty

No:
- Wants a guaranteed #1 in ChatGPT by Friday
- Would rather wait until it is proven and crowded
- Brand new site, no business or history behind it
- Wants a report to file, not changes to make

## voice.md

### How the business talks

Two registers, deliberately different, because they do different jobs.

**Sales page — Alex Becker / Hyros direct response.** Short punchy paragraphs, a
bold one-line hammer after a block of setup, a named mechanism (the four
signals), an explicit us-versus-them comparison, blunt objection handling
including admitting what cannot be guaranteed, and a qualification section that
tells some readers not to buy.

**Email sequence — André Chaperon.** One serialised story told in order, not 22
self-contained tips. Open loops: 19 of 22 emails end on something they do not
close. Nothing to click for the first 19 emails. The offer arrives at email 20,
once, as a text link. Written to one person reading on a phone between jobs.

**Reports** — plain, specific, unpatronising. Written for the owner, never in
the scanner's vocabulary.

### How it should never sound

- Hyped. No exclamation marks anywhere in the sequence, and the one that exists
  is inside quoted copy being mocked.
- Like a newsletter. The email shell strips the coloured canvas, the masthead,
  the horizontal rule and the padded button, because a button exists in exactly
  one kind of email.
- Like an agency. No "strategy retainers", no ninety-page audits.
- Certain about things nobody can be certain about.
- Like a chatbot. See the humanizer notes below.

### Phrases it uses

- "Named or not named."
- "You did not lose. You were never entered."
- "Vague businesses are not disliked. They are unrecommendable."
- "Level is a good place to be. It is not the same as being the one that gets named."
- "It is not rewarding the best operator. It is rewarding the best-documented one."
- "Silence does not read as neutral. It reads as unproven."
- "There is one answer."

### Phrases it avoids

- Em dashes. Reduced from 72 to 5 across all customer-facing copy; the five
  survivors are price separators inside buttons and one plain-text divider.
- British spelling. US throughout: optimize, not optimise.
- "Not just X, it's Y" and its relatives. Cut from 9 to 5 because the shape
  became audible.
- Overused AI vocabulary: delve, crucial, pivotal, tapestry, testament,
  showcase, vibrant, robust, seamless, leverage.
- Sales-brochure language: nestled, boasts, breathtaking, must-visit.
- Anything the humanizer flags as an inflated-importance construction.

### Examples of good writing

**Sales page, the hammer:**
> If that name is your competitor, you did not come second.
> You were not in the conversation at all.

**Sales page, honest objection handling:**
> No. Nobody can, and anyone who tells you otherwise is either lying to you or
> does not understand what they are selling.

**Email 1, the story open:**
> Then, because it seemed like the honest thing to do before pointing it at
> anyone else, I ran it on footholdsystems.com. Twenty out of a hundred. Grade F.

**Email 17, admitting an error:**
> A friend ran his site through it. The report told him his business name did
> not bring up his website. It was not true.

**Report, the cold-search caveat:**
> How this was measured: a cold search. No login, no history, no location, no
> personalisation. Search your own name in your own browser and you will very
> likely see yourself first. Both results are real. This is the one an AI sees.

### House rules

Short sentences. Plain English. No hype. No exclamation marks. No em dashes. US
spelling. Anything technical must be copy-pasteable or it does not belong.
Assume a busy owner reading on a phone between jobs.
