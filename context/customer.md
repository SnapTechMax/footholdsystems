# FootHold — Customer

*ICP, pains, objections and decision criteria. Mostly assumed.*

> [!info] How to read this
> **Known** = observed in the code or stated directly by Max.
> **Assumed** = a hypothesis that reached live copy without customer research.
> `[UNSURE]` = genuinely unknown, left blank rather than guessed at.

**Everything in this section is assumed unless marked otherwise.** It comes from
copy that Max approved, not from talking to buyers. Treat it as the current
hypothesis and overwrite it the moment real customers say otherwise.

### ICP

Owner-operators and small marketing decision-makers who already have a working
business and a website that predates any of this. Someone who has spent money on
SEO or ads before and can tell the difference between a channel and a gimmick.

Sharpest fit: a local service business with real margin, real reviews, and a
site built for humans by somebody who is no longer involved.

`[UNSURE]` Company size, revenue band, geography beyond an implied US/California
default, industry concentration.

### Pains

- Leads quietly declining with no diagnosable cause.
- Having paid for SEO for years and not knowing whether it still works.
- Watching a competitor get named and having no idea why.
- Being unable to answer "is this a real channel or hype?"
- No time. Everything on the fix list is easy and none of it is urgent, which is
  exactly why it never gets done.

### Objections

Answered directly on the sales page:

1. *"Isn't this too early?"*
2. *"Can you guarantee ChatGPT will recommend me?"* — answered **no**, on purpose.
3. *"I already rank number one on Google."*
4. *"Won't AI just work out who is genuinely best?"*
5. *"Can't I just do this myself?"* — answered **yes, partly**, on purpose.

**Observed, not assumed:** a real reader disputed his scan result because he
ranks #1 for his own name and the report implied otherwise. The objection
"your tool is wrong about me" is live and has already happened once.

### Buying triggers

`[UNSURE]` — none observed. Reasonable hypotheses only:

- Reading their own scan and seeing a competitor named instead of them.
- A grade lower than expected on a site they are proud of.
- Someone mentioning they found a competitor via ChatGPT.

### Questions they ask

From the site FAQ, so assumed:

- What exactly do I get from the free scan?
- How long does it take to see movement?
- Do I have to stop doing SEO?
- Does this work for local service businesses?
- Is there a contract?

### Language

`[UNSURE]` — no transcripts, no recordings, no support inbox to mine. This is
the largest genuine gap in this document. The copy currently uses language
written *for* the customer rather than language captured *from* them.

What is known: the report deliberately avoids Ora's engineer-facing vocabulary,
because "publish an Agentic Resource Discovery catalog" means nothing to a
roofer.

### Fears

- Paying for something unmeasurable.
- Being sold hype by another agency.
- Being told to rebuild a website they just paid for.
- Looking foolish for not having heard of this.

### Decision criteria

- Can they see the problem themselves? The scan exists to make it self-evident.
- Is the number credible? A grade contradicted by their own experience destroys
  trust instantly, which is why the grade cap and the cold-search caveat exist.
- Do they keep the work? Both domains and a written record of what changed.
- Is it a retainer? The $1,500 is deliberately one-off.
