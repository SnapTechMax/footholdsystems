# FootHold — Company

*What FootHold sells, who it serves, and what it believes*

> [!info] How to read this
> **Known** = observed in the code or stated directly by Max.
> **Assumed** = a hypothesis that reached live copy without customer research.
> `[UNSURE]` = genuinely unknown, left blank rather than guessed at.

### What the company sells

Answer Engine Optimization. The one-line offer, in Max's words:

> I help your business show up on ChatGPT when potential customers ask.

Concretely: making a business findable, readable and recommendable to AI
assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, Google AI Overviews) at the
moment someone describes a problem and asks who to hire.

Entry point is a **free AI visibility scan**. Someone submits a URL, an email
and a business category; the site scans them, scores them, and emails a
plain-English report. The paid tiers sell the fixes and then the implementation.

### Who it serves

Three categories are built into the product, and each is scored against a
different check set:

| Category | Who |
|---|---|
| `sbo` | Local and service businesses: trades, clinics, agencies, restaurants |
| `ecommerce` | Sells physical or digital products online |
| `saas` | Sells software, an app, or an API |

`sbo` is the default on the form and the assumed majority of cold traffic.

**Assumed** (from approved sales copy, not research): businesses with real
margin where one customer is worth hundreds or thousands rather than twelve
dollars; customers who research before buying; an established business with
real reviews and history.

### How it makes money

Three tiers. Detail in `offer.md`. Summary: **$49** for the fix list, **$1,497**
for implementation plus a purpose-built second domain, **$4,500 + $2,500/month
on a six-month minimum** for the ongoing retainer.

The retainer is a **separate funnel**, sold only to people who have already
bought the $1,497. It must not appear in top-of-funnel copy.

`[UNSURE]` Whether any tier has been sold yet. No revenue figures, no customer
count, no delivery-capacity constraints are known.

### What it believes

These are load-bearing. Most of the copy follows from them.

- **Search is going winner-take-all.** Google returned ten results and fourth
  place still got clicks. An assistant returns one recommendation. You are not
  second; you are absent.
- **Models recommend entities, not pages.** Ranking #1 on Google does not make
  a model believe you are the business to call. Document matching and reputation
  are different problems.
- **A model does not trust your website about your website.** It weighs
  corroboration. Silence does not read as neutral, it reads as unproven.
- **Vague businesses are unrecommendable.** Not disliked. Unmatched to any
  question.
- **Nobody controls a model's output.** Anyone guaranteeing a ranking is lying.
  What is controllable is every input the model uses.
- **The loss is invisible.** No impression, no bounce, no line in Analytics. The
  first marketing channel that is undetectable to the business losing it.
- **Give the tactics away.** The 22-email sequence hands over every technical
  fix for free. The value sold is judgement and implementation, not information.

### What makes it different

- **The second domain.** The $1,497 builds a separate site whose only audience
  is machines. An existing site has to sell to people and carry a brand, and
  every one of those pulls against machine readability. Max's framing: nobody
  else is doing this.
- **Category-specific scoring.** Most tools score every site against one list.
  Scoring a party rental company on whether it ships an SDK is nonsense; scoring
  a SaaS company without it is flattery.
- **A free scan that is genuinely useful**, delivered before any ask.
- **`.gov` backlinks** in the retainer. Max's framing: "no one is doing this."
- **A refund-scale guarantee** on the retainer: not ranking on LLM engines after
  180 days means Max pays $15,000.
